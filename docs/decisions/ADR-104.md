# ADR-104 · A per-brand ambiguity flag in the country pack (2026-10-31, Claude Code)

**Context.** Real-test incident (case 11, audio, scam 0.92): the transcript said "dian" lowercase, and `_brand_in_body` discards every lowercase match so "claro" ("sure") isn't mistaken for the Claro brand. The reply came out with no "this isn't from DIAN" and no official channel. In audio, capitalization is an ASR artifact ("DIAN" and "Dian" across two runs of the same audio); in text the same happens with "I got something from bancolombia".

**Options.** (a) ignore capitalization only for audio — leaves lowercase text broken and ties case kind into brand attribution. (b) `ambigua: true` per brand in the pack; only those keep the lowercase rule. (c) a hardcoded list of common words — duplicates knowledge that belongs to the country pack.

**Decision.** (b). Ambiguous in CO: Dale, Coordinadora, Envía, Deprisa, Claro, Mercado Libre, Éxito (ordinary Spanish words/phrases); in US: Chase, UPS, Apple. Everything else (DIAN, Bancolombia, Nequi, SIMIT…) is recognized regardless of case. Default `false`: a new brand that happens to be a common word must be flagged on the way in.

**What would invalidate it.** A legit eval case mistakenly naming a non-ambiguous brand in lowercase (add it to ambiguous), or a scam with a lowercase ambiguous brand left with no official channel (switch to context instead of a flag).

**Addendum (reviewer/judge).** Amazon stays non-ambiguous (in a scam, "amazon" is the store); "4-72" has no letters and already always matched. Open gap: an ambiguous brand capitalized at the start of a sentence ("Claro que sí", ASR) still gets named; closed only if it shows up in evals.
