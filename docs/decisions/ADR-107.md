# ADR-107 · With several brands in the body, the wallet or gateway is the payment rail and the impersonated one is the other brand (2026-10-31, Claude Code)

**Context.** Incident Oct 31, case 14 (voice note): "this is the DIAN … debt … pay via Nequi" replied "not from Nequi" and gave Nequi's channel. `_brand_in_body` walked `pack.brands` in JSON order and returned the first match: Nequi sits at position 20 of CO.json, DIAN at 28. The pack's order means nothing.

**Options.** (a) first brand in text order. (b) priority by type: a brand of kind `billetera` (wallet) or `pagos` (payments) is the rail when another brand is named; the impersonated one is the first non-rail brand; if only rails are named, the first one named. (c) also require a payment pattern next to the wallet to treat it as a rail. (d) don't choose: report both roles.

**Decision.** (b) + (d). `build._brands_in_body` returns brands in text order, `_impersonated_in_body` takes the first non-rail one (`RAIL_KINDS = {"billetera", "pagos"}`), and the header becomes `verdict.scam.brand_rail`: "impersonates {brand} and asks for payment to a {rail} account". `impersonated_brand` and `official_channel` are the impersonated brand's (dian.gov.co). The rail is also reported when the impersonated brand comes from a hard signal (a link mimicking the DIAN + "pay via Nequi"). No new field on `Verdict`: the rail lives only in the text code assembles. No length cap in specs/05 and WhatsApp accepts 4096 characters, so the sentence fits.

**Why not (c).** The pack already treats naming Nequi or Daviplata as a payment pattern (`pago_personal`, `cuenta_bancaria`), so requiring the pattern would be circular: it would always hold. "I'm from Nequi, confirm your PIN" has no other brand and Nequi stays impersonated (c-0231).

**What would invalidate it.** A scam that impersonates the wallet and names another non-rail brand only in passing ("Nequi: your payment to Claro failed, confirm your PIN") — Claro would win. And a rail named in a credentials theft alongside another brand would read "asks for payment" (flagged with ponytail in `_header`).

**Addendum (Oct 31).** The rule only ran over body brands: a hard signal from the analyst naming Nequi won unfiltered. Now brands from hard signals with `source="analyst"` go through `_impersonated` together with body brands (analyst first). Brands from the detector (impersonating link, lookalike) still win unfiltered (rule 5): "Nequi: your payment to the DIAN failed, confirm your PIN at nequi-seguro.co" still says Nequi; folding them into the same filter broke it (caught by the judge). Finding: the real analyst (`analyst/loop.py:_signals`) today only emits light signals with no `brand`, so the change is defensive; the DIAN + "pay via Nequi" case already came out right from body order (c-0230). The rail still only comes from body brands: a wallet brought only by the analyst is never asserted as a rail (D-065; also caught by the judge). Same rule, not a new decision.
